# Omnia: current architecture and public positioning

OS_VISUAL_REBUILD.md is the latest controlling brief; this internal reference describes the corresponding architecture. This describes the intended product architecture, not a claim of implemented runtime functionality or deployed agents.

## Company and product

Omnia is a technology company building Omnia OS. Omnia OS is its central offering: a shared, extensible software foundation integrated into each business's existing technological environment. Omnia handles the integration, configuration, engineering, deployment and ongoing technical complexity. Clients bring objectives, problems, responsibilities and ideas; they do not need to operate the infrastructure themselves.

## Omnia OS

- Shared technological foundation and Module infrastructure.
- Native intelligence and agent runtime.
- Business context and knowledge, reasoning and intelligent coordination.
- Agent creation and management, memory and persistent responsibilities where appropriate.
- Secure, scoped access to connected information and tools.
- Configuration, permissions, approvals, human escalation, coordination and deployment.
- Communication and coordination between agents where appropriate.

AI is native to the architecture. Individual Solutions may use non-AI capabilities, including deterministic workflows, connections, extensions and purpose-built functionality.

## Omnia Modules and four internal types

Modules are a public capability ecosystem: they can be installed, combined, configured, extended or purpose-built. The categories below remain internal engineering terminology, not four mandatory products.

1. Connectors connect external software, data and services.
2. Extensions add functionality inside existing software.
3. Automations coordinate explicit processes and actions across systems.
4. Custom Services provide purpose-built backend functionality and other capabilities.

AI Agents are not a fifth Module type. They are created and operated through the native intelligence layer. They can use appropriate Modules as tools and capabilities, within a configured responsibility, context and permissions.

## Agents

A responsibility is investigated and configured, not treated as an unrestricted prompt. Define relevant business context, permitted systems and events, what can be prepared or carried out, persistence/memory needs, approval boundaries, escalation and coordination with other agents. Financial, legal, sensitive and consequential actions require carefully designed permissions and human oversight. Intelligent assistance in creating additional agents is a possible future direction, not a shipped public feature.

## Solutions

A Solution combines appropriate existing Modules, agents, configuration and necessary custom engineering through Omnia OS. The foundation is reusable and extensible; implementations are not unrelated products rebuilt from scratch. Initial repeatable offerings do not define the ecosystem's long-term limits.

## Context and scope

Semantic retrieval/vector databases can provide access to permitted indexed documents, policies and product knowledge. Connectors and services provide current structured records from connected business systems; document indexes do not represent a perfect live copy of every system. Modules expose authorized tools. The OS supplies the intended common framework for identity, permissions, audit and coordination.

Internal and customer-facing agents can have distinct knowledge, data scopes, actions, autonomy, approval paths and oversight. Customer-facing agents reference approved public knowledge and only a verified customer's own permitted context. Restricted internal information and other customers' records remain outside that scope.

## Frontend and backend breadth

Omnia OS can support extensions within existing tools, entirely new employee workspaces, customer-facing websites and portals, purpose-built applications, backend services and custom business logic. A Solution can use existing interfaces, new interfaces, or both. The architecture does not restrict Omnia to backend integrations.
