# Replacing the retail story

The owner explicitly requires one substantial interactive story, surrounded by a complete company introduction. The story is not the company model, a fixed product, or a set of industry offerings.

## Concepts considered

| Concept | Emotional impact | Clarity | Discovery | Credibility and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A hotel collection becomes one personal stay | Strong: people can imagine the experience directly | Strong: a guest, a group, and a desired stay | Cross-property service becomes one experience, then adapts to the guest's intent and a changed day | Plausible with connected operations, custom guest/staff experiences and business-approved service rules; no real availability is implied |
| A training company creates learning around each client's real work | Moderate: strong business case, less immediate emotional pull | Needs more explanation of learning and delivery | Customer, learner and delivery experiences can form a new branded offering | Credible as a tailored digital service; familiar learning software makes the central reveal less distinctive |
| A professional firm turns expertise into a continuing client service | Moderate | Can become abstract or too close to an ordinary client portal | Information and expert input could create a new ongoing service | Scope and professional judgment require care; weaker as the major visual story |
| An events company creates a service shaped around each group | Strong | Strong | Guest wishes, operations and suppliers move together | Risks becoming a quotation or booking workflow, close to earlier weak examples |

These are editorial judgments, not research findings or performance scores.

## Selected story: one personal stay across a hotel collection

**Ambition:** A hotel group wants guests to experience the whole collection as one stay, shaped around what matters to them.

**Beginning:** The group imagines an offering that reaches across properties and teams. This establishes a new service, not an existing task to automate.

**Transformation:** Step into the guest's stay. Choose time to switch off or time together. The guest-facing experience and map change, showing how one shared intention can shape places and service across the collection.

**Discovery:** Change the day: it rains. The guest's intent is retained. A family picnic can become a private family workshop at a sister property, shaped around ages and interests; a slow garden breakfast can move to a quiet lounge. The host confirms the alternative, and the relevant teams receive the changed context. The guest does not need to reconstruct the stay with each separate team. The group can offer one personal stay that reaches beyond the property where the room happens to be.

**Payoff:** Reveal what Omnia would bring together: existing property/service tools, a custom guest experience, cross-property service rules, and coordinated staff context. The business gains a new connected-stay offering; the software takes the shape needed to deliver it.

No real hotel, partner, guest, availability, price, booking or deployed solution is represented. The user explores an illustrative possibility. Omnia is coming soon.

## Placement in the wider narrative

1. Opening: state the complete company proposition.
2. Company model: explain what a business engages Omnia to do and the scope of what can be created.
3. Immersive story: show a new business capability in a hotel collection.
4. Range: show customer-facing experiences, internal quoting capabilities, and technology behind a new learning service. These are different outcomes, not industry cards or product categories.
5. Existing technology: establish global, regional and company-specific reach.
6. Working relationship: investigation, agreed scope, engineering, testing, operation and adaptation; optional Omnia OS depth.
7. Participation: preserve real inquiry and launch-interest infrastructure.
