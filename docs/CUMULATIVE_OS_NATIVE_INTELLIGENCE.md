## 1. The correct Omnia architecture

The public-facing product identity has three main concepts.

**Omnia OS** — The persistent, customizable, AI-native foundation that works with a business's existing technology.

**Omnia Modules** — The expandable ecosystem of capabilities that can be installed, combined, configured, extended, or purpose-built. Modules are an important part of the product's marketing, not merely internal engineering terminology.

**Solutions** — Combinations of Omnia OS, Modules, native AI, and custom engineering that deliver an organization's desired capabilities.

Internally, the four Module categories remain Connectors, Extensions, Automations, and Custom Services.

AI Agents are not a fifth Module type.

**AI is built into Omnia OS itself.**

Modules give the environment additional capabilities and reach. The native intelligence layer allows AI to work with the connected environment and perform appropriate responsibilities through those capabilities.

Don't depict AI as an optional chatbot installed on top of an otherwise unrelated integration platform.

But don't depict Omnia OS as nothing more than AI either.

## 3. Internal AI agents versus customer-facing AI agents

The states are:

**For your organization**

**For your customers**

Switching between them should reveal genuinely different applications of the same native intelligence foundation.

The underlying Omnia OS should remain visually consistent.

**An agent for the way you work.**

Supporting copy:

"From individual responsibilities to entire teams, Omnia agents can be configured around how your organization operates.

Give an agent the appropriate knowledge, connected capabilities, and responsibilities. It can retrieve information, monitor activity, prepare work, coordinate permitted actions, and bring important decisions back to the right people."

**Operations Intelligence**

Heading:

**Your intelligence. Your customer experience.**

## 5. Explain the shared intelligence advantage accurately

One reason AI is native to Omnia OS is that agents can work with the connected environment rather than being limited to one application.

Give this concept its own brief reveal inside the AI screen.

### Heading

**Context across your entire environment.**

Explain in plain English:

"An agent doesn't have to begin with an isolated set of information. Omnia can make relevant knowledge and connected business capabilities available through one shared foundation, so agents can understand more context and perform more useful work."

The technical concept behind this is:

- Vector databases and retrieval systems can provide semantic access to permitted documents, policies, product knowledge, and other indexed information.
- Connectors and services provide access to current structured records and live business data.
- Modules expose functionality agents may use as authorized tools.
- The OS supplies a common framework for permissions, identity, auditing, and coordination.

**Do not describe vector databases as holding a perfect live copy of every business system.**

The architecture should support system-wide integration while enforcing access boundaries.

An agent should see **the parts of the connected environment it is permitted to see**, not unrestricted organizational data.

Different agents can have different scopes, knowledge sources, actions, approval requirements, and levels of autonomy.

This is a key part of Omnia's enterprise-grade customization.

Keep this technical explanation discreet in the main marketing experience. The visual should communicate the idea first.

## Final standard

Visitors should understand that Omnia OS is **not simply AI software**.

It's a deeply customizable, extensible technological foundation that can connect and improve an organization's existing systems.

Omnia Modules let that environment continually gain new capabilities.

Its native intelligence layer makes the connected environment useful to highly configurable AI agents—both for people inside the organization and for the customers it serves.

Those agents can be given relevant knowledge, specialized responsibilities, access to permitted capabilities, and organization-specific boundaries.

**The OS gives the agents context. The Modules give them capabilities. The organization defines how they're allowed to operate.**
