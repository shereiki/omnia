# Content and discovery revision

## Rendered starting point

Reviewed the current site in Chromium at 1440px and 390px before editing. The visual identity is retained. The service-configuration diagram was a weak proof of the broader idea: it described an ordinary booking-style capability. The opening did not explicitly identify Omnia as a technology company. The proprietary-software paragraph used technical language while the optional foundation ended in internal risk language. Those issues weakened customer understanding.

## Narrative decision

Keep the five-section architecture and the established violet/frost/lavender/mint direction. Establish the company and its relationship to the client's ambition in the opening. Develop one ordinary customer request into a larger operational situation rather than add more product examples or feature categories.

The visitor moves through three chapters:

1. A customer sends a photo and asks for something similar by tomorrow.
2. The nearest store has no suitable stock. Looking across product knowledge, other locations, incoming stock, delivery and people creates a wider set of options for approval.
3. An incoming delivery slips. Connecting the delay to customer promises lets a team see affected orders, alternatives and prepared updates before the issue reaches customers.

Each advance changes the business situation and the available response. The illustration shows physical stores and customer promises, not application logos, fabricated dashboards, metrics, or a backend architecture. The sequence is explicitly illustrative. Nothing is being matched to a real catalog, and no live business systems are queried.

The initial expandable-text prototype was rejected during rendered review because it still felt like an explanation. The implemented storyboard uses a changing situation, accompanying illustration and a prepared outcome. Back navigation lets a reader compare perspectives; there is no replay control or selector for content already visible.

The conclusion of the story offers an editable inquiry starter. Existing visitor text is preserved. Choosing this link does not send an inquiry; the real form submission is unchanged.

## Meaning contributed by the remaining sections

- Opening: what Omnia is, why the business can start with an outcome, and who handles the technology.
- Discovery: the same request becomes a coordinated response, then an opportunity to anticipate a problem.
- Ecosystem: familiar and company-specific software are starting points, not reasons to abandon the idea. Technical jargon was translated into practical meaning. Existing brand assets are unchanged.
- Approach: work backward from the goal, combine existing software with newly built capabilities, and check real feasibility and approvals with the team. This explains flexibility beyond the retail illustration.
- Invitation: visitors can describe how they wish their business worked, with no technical brief required.

Omnia OS remains optional. Its language defines the foundation and intended offering; it does not present a scalability argument or a technical-risk disclaimer. Coming-soon status and illustrative-story labeling establish the relevant public facts.
